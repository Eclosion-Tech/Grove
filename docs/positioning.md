# Grove positioning — direction, 2026-09-06

This direction was refined and approved in the Grove discussion. It is not a claim of proven market demand. See [admin platform](admin-platform.md) for the accepted extension and implementation boundary.

**Grove gives developers the power to build a workspace their clients can confidently operate.**

Developers define exactly which content, design controls, application data and actions users can access. Visual editing, code, CLI, API and future AI operations respect the same boundaries. The CMS is the first complete application on a broader administration foundation; application-specific modules extend that workspace without taking ownership of every connected system.

The first audience is independent developers and small studios delivering bespoke content-driven client sites, with clients who need straightforward ongoing editing. The proposed value is less work delivering the next site, fewer support calls after launch, and safer maintenance as shared foundations evolve. Broad appeal can grow from solving this repeatable workflow well.

## Reasons to choose it that we need to earn

1. A client understands their workspace immediately: their business vocabulary, useful defaults, a small set of relevant tasks, real-site preview, and clear effects of changing shared content.
2. A studio reuses proven schemas/components without recreating admin wiring for each client. Versioned upgrades preserve local content and customizations, with a preview, change report, migration plan and recovery path.
3. Handoff is practical: documented data/components, predictable operating costs, explicit ownership, and a tested path to another maintainer or hosting arrangement. A Postgres database alone does not fulfill that promise.

Some foundations exist: independent package/host separation, code-owned schemas, site-owned Puck components, revision checks, history, scoped references and usage tracking. Reusable project kits, safe fleet-wide upgrades, production identity, portable exports, tested recovery, handoff tooling and pricing remain work to design and validate. Do not market them as delivered.

## Competitive reality

- [Sanity](https://www.sanity.io/docs/visual-editing) already offers structured content with real-site visual editing, draft previews and click/drag workflows. Visual editing plus a headless API is not a distinctive claim.
- [WordPress](https://wordpress.org/about/features/) brings familiar publishing, media, revisions, extensibility, themes and a large plugin/community ecosystem. Grove will need to justify adoption for a tailored client workflow; it does not currently replace that ecosystem.
- [Payload](https://payloadcms.com/) is a particularly relevant comparison: code-based configuration, agency positioning, white labeling and [multi-tenancy](https://payloadcms.com/multi-tenancy). Code-first, self-hosted, or multi-tenant alone will not distinguish Grove.

These are observations from current primary product sources, not comparative benchmarks. The hypothesis is that a carefully integrated studio-to-client workflow can be compelling even when individual features exist elsewhere. We should compare the total setup and maintenance work for the same representative site.

## How to test the position

Use the next few real client projects to measure time to a working editor and handoff, unassisted completion of ordinary client tasks, support requests after launch, and effort/risk of deploying a shared improvement. Ask an independent studio to reproduce the workflow from documentation. Their friction is better evidence of general appeal than our familiarity with our own tools.

Keep foundational record/image editing strong now. Prioritize a complete reusable client-site kit and trustworthy publishing/handoff workflow before adding a broad marketplace or unrelated platform features. Optional AI can later operate on the same attributable, reviewable draft lifecycle; it is not a substitute for the product promise.
